Important details and pitfalls
PhysioNet/CinC 2015

[Established] Of the 750 public alarms, 458 are labeled false and 292 true. This is about 61% false within the selected challenge samples, not a population alarm rate. The dataset includes asystole, extreme bradycardia, extreme tachycardia, ventricular tachycardia, and ventricular fibrillation/flutter. 

physionet.org
+1

[Established] The challenge intentionally separates selected alarms by at least five minutes, limits how many alarms come from each patient, and excludes many ordinary monitoring intervals. Events earlier in a record are not comprehensively annotated. The original hidden patient-disjoint test set is not available for your own evaluation. 

physionet.org

This has three consequences:

[Established] You can calculate sensitivity to the included true alarms and the proportion of included false alarms you defer.

[Likely] You cannot estimate actual alerts per astronaut-day from these records.

[Likely] If you split the 750 records randomly, you cannot confidently claim patient-independent generalization without verifying patient group assignments.

MIT-BIH Noise Stress Test

[Established] It starts from two ECG recordings with known beats and adds controlled electrode-motion noise. This permits measuring how ECG algorithms degrade as SNR deteriorates. It does not automatically produce ground-truth true versus false device alarms unless you also run and evaluate a defined detector. 

physionet.org

PPG-DaLiA

[Established] The wrist data include 64 Hz blood-volume-pulse measurements and 32 Hz accelerometry, with higher-frequency chest ECG available as a reference. The published dataset is intended for heart-rate estimation under daily activities and motion artifacts. 

archive.ics.uci.edu

[Likely] For the exercise-focused branch of your idea, a reasonable metric is heart-rate estimation error compared with ECG, stratified by activity and accepted signal coverage. It is not honest to convert heart-rate error directly into a missed-arrhythmia probability.

WESAD and TROIKA license caution

[Established] WESAD's original author-hosted page explicitly limits usage to scientific, non-commercial purposes; UCI directs readers to that original site. For TROIKA, the Zenodo metadata reports an open license, while the dataset description speaks specifically of challenge or academic research use. 

ubi29.informatik.uni-siegen.de
+2

Best dataset choice for 24 hours
[Likely] Use PhysioNet 2015 as the primary safety benchmark if the aim is a false-alarm-versus-miss curve. Use a small MIT-BIH Noise Stress Test subset to demonstrate what happens as signal quality deteriorates.

4. Critique: why NASA judges might reject the idea
I would expect these objections from a skeptical flight surgeon or space-medicine researcher.

Objection 1 — Your safety trade-off is not medically authorized
[Established] The 2015 PhysioNet challenge uses a scoring formula that makes incorrectly dismissing a true alarm five times as costly as retaining a false one. That reflects the asymmetric risk in the benchmark, although the factor of five is a challenge scoring decision, not a universal clinical safety standard. 

physionet.org

[Likely] A flight surgeon would ask: What clinical evidence permits the system to defer a suspected serious cardiac event because the astronaut is exercising?

Your answer cannot be that motion makes the measurement unreliable. A true arrhythmia can coexist with exercise and poor sensor quality.

Recommendation: Do not allow a user-adjustable notification budget to override safety-critical alerts. Use the budget visualization for evaluating candidate policies, not as an operational medical safety control.

Objection 2 — Deferring is not automatically safer than suppressing
[Likely] An alert that is retained in a ledger but never reviewed has still failed to reach the operator when it mattered. Logging a decision does not undo a harmful delay.

A legitimate deferral policy needs an explicit deadline, escalation path, and measurable delay to review. Alarm priority should depend on the consequences of delayed response, consistent with the risk-based framework established by IEC 60601-1-8. 

iso.org

Objection 3 — Your evaluation combines incompatible problems
[Established] PhysioNet 2015 concerns selected critical arrhythmia alarms, while PPG-DaLiA concerns heart-rate estimation during activities. Neither dataset provides the combined ground truth of astronaut exercise, sensor artifacts, emergency events and actual notification outcomes. 

physionet.org
+1

[Likely] If you train on one and report an overall astronaut-safety performance metric, the result would not be scientifically justified.

Objection 4 — Signal quality is not clinical normality
[Established] The ECG quality literature uses agreement between detectors and physiological plausibility checks. These methods estimate signal trustworthiness; they do not independently establish the patient's clinical condition. 

pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

[Likely] The correct interface requires three conceptual states: an interpretable measurement, an interpretable concerning measurement, and an insufficiently reliable measurement. Poor-quality data should lead to verification or escalation as appropriate, not a green status.

Objection 5 — You may increase the workload you claim to reduce
[Likely] If your system generates a signal-quality warning, asks for a second measurement, creates a deferred event, and then reminds someone to review it, you've potentially replaced one alert with four tasks.

NASA's EIMO task-load work explicitly warns against inadvertently increasing the workload imposed by medical-support systems. 

ntrs.nasa.gov

Objection 6 — The space adaptation is superficial
[Likely] A generic ICU classifier with an astronaut-themed dashboard would be weak under the challenge's Relevance and Validity criteria. Your application needs to show what changes when Earth cannot provide immediate medical assistance. 

spaceappschallenge.org
+1

Show the difference between local crew decisions and delayed flight-surgeon review, including what information is recorded for a later handover.

Objection 7 — Even an algorithm that performs well may not be novel
[Established] Signal-quality-based false-alarm detection was the explicit subject of a public PhysioNet challenge in 2015, including published code and winning solutions. 

physionet.org

[Likely] Without a meaningful human-factors improvement, your project could look like a simplified reproduction of established research.

Objection 8 — There is no evaluated human benefit
[Guess] This could be your biggest presentation weakness. If you show only a curve of classified alarms, judges may ask where the astronaut's ability to evaluate and act on their health has actually improved.

Recommendation: Include a small, explicitly preliminary usability experiment measuring correct next-step selection and time to action, not merely interface aesthetics.

Proposed interface rules
[Likely] Use multimodal delivery with different behavior for different information needs:

Critical event: salient audio plus visual message, optional haptic redundancy, explicit acknowledgment, and no silent automatic dismissal.

Measurement unreliable: explain the specific quality concern and provide a direct path to retry or use another available measurement.

Low-priority review: persist the event in a visible queue with a deadline and escalation rule.

Hands-free interaction: allow spoken presentation of the warning and next step, with a physical or conventional interface fallback.

These are design proposals, not validated spaceflight procedures. The underlying principles are supported by medical-alarm human-factors research and the IEC risk-priority framework.
