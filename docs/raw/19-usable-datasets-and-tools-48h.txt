Given your team's strengths in biosignal processing and human-computer interaction (HCI), the most effective 48-hour architecture combines four layers that were recently validated by NASA's own Artemis II Human Research Data Methodology Challenge (which wrapped in July 2026):
Continuous Biosignal & Artifact-Gating Layer (Explicitly Labeled Proxy + Real Hardware Specs):

Pull OSD-575 (Inspiration4 Blood Serum Comprehensive Metabolic Panel and Immune/Cardiac Cytokines) and OSD-656 (Urine Inflammation Panel), or use the pre-aligned $24 \times 1112$ Inspiration4 Master Table (OSD-569 through OSD-687) via the OSDR REST API or AWS S3 (aws s3 ls --no-sign-request s3://nasa-osdr/).

3. What Is NOT Available Publicly (And Cannot Be Faked Honestly)
To score high on Validity in NASA Space Apps judging, you must state clearly when you use proxy data and never claim to possess the following restricted datasets:
Individual Career Astronaut Medical Records (LSAH / LSDA): De-identified individual astronaut records require a formal research proposal and $\sim 13\text{ months}$ of lead time; attributable (identifiable) records require full NASA IRB and LSAH Advisory Board approval with a $\sim 25\text{-month}$ lead time under HIPAA, the Privacy Act of 1974, and OMB M-06-16. Only pooled summary statistics and Medical Requirements Integration Documents (MRIDs) are public on the NASA Life Sciences Portal.
Raw Human Genomic / RNA Sequencing Reads (FASTQ/BAM) on OSDR: While processed gene-expression counts, cytokine arrays, and metabolic CSVs for Inspiration4 are open-access without login, raw astronaut sequence files are restricted behind OSDR's controlled-access system to prevent genetic re-identification.
Live or Archived Artemis II Astronaut Biomedical Data: Although the 2026 NASA Artemis II Human Research Data Methodology Challenge focused on the four-astronaut Artemis II study suite (ARCHeR, Immune Biomarkers, and Standard Measures), no actual Artemis II crew data was released to participants; the challenge operated strictly on public proxy datasets (such as Inspiration4 and PhysioNet MMASH). Similarly, public Artemis II real-time web trackers (such as TrialX's dashboard) explicitly display simulated health vitals based on mission phases rather than live astronaut telemetry.
Continuous In-Flight ISS Bio-Monitor / Astroskin Raw Waveforms: Although the Canadian Space Agency's Bio-Monitor (Astroskin) has recorded $250\text{ Hz}$ ECG and $125\text{ Hz}$ respiration aboard the ISS and in HERA analogs, raw astronaut waveform streams are downlinked to private ground servers and PI repositories rather than open public buckets. Use the published Astroskin sampling specifications paired with an honest terrestrial waveform proxy (e.g., PhysioNet MMASH).
Raw Crew Psychological Diaries, Private Medical Conferences (PMCs), and Audio/Video Logs: Individual psychological logs from ISS, CHAPEA, SIRIUS, HI-SEAS, and Mars-500 are strictly protected under human-subject confidentiality rules; only aggregate compliance, actigraphy epochs, and Likert acceptability summaries are published.
